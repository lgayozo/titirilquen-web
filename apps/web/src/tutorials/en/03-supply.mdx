# 2. Supply

Three distinct submodules, united by one idea: **travel time depends on assigned flow**.

## Car (Greenshields + BPR)

Per-lane capacity derived from jam density:

$$
Q = \frac{v_l \cdot k_e}{4}, \quad k_e = \frac{1000}{l + \text{gap}}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de autos" />

Per-segment time under congestion (Bureau of Public Roads):

$$
t_i = t_{i_0} \left( 1 + \alpha \left( \frac{q_i}{N_p \cdot Q} \right)^\beta \right)
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de autos" />

where $q_i$ is the flow accumulated toward the CBD, $N_p$ the number of lanes and $t_{i_0} = \frac{L/N}{v_l}$ the free-flow segment time.

## Bicycle (BPR with slope and physical floor)

Slope adjustment factor:

$$
f_p = \begin{cases} -0.0579\,p + 0.9992 & p > 0 \\ -0.0455\,p + 1 & p \le 0 \end{cases}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de bicicletas" tag="R-1" />

From $f_p$ two speeds are derived (left and right of the CBD), clamped to $[5, 45]$ km/h. The per-segment time applies the BPR **capped at the time to walk the segment**:

$$
t_i = \min\left\{ t_{i_0} \left( 1 + \alpha \left( \frac{q_i}{Q} \right)^\beta \right),\ \frac{L/N}{v_c} \cdot 60 \right\}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de bicicletas" tag="R-7" />

<Callout type="info">
The floor is physical: a congested bike lane can never be slower than **dismounting and pushing** the bike ($v_c$ = walking speed). Without the cap, the accumulated BPR produced absurd times in the congested periphery.
</Callout>

## Metro (cyclic system)

The operating frequency is sized to the **critical link**:

$$
f_{\text{op}} = \min\!\left(f_{\max},\ \max\!\left(f_{\min},\ \frac{L_{\max}}{K}\right)\right)
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de trenes" />

where $L_{\max}$ is the maximum observed flow and $K$ the per-train capacity. The endogenous frequency induces the **Mohring effect**: more demand ⇒ higher frequency ⇒ less waiting for everyone (transit's economy of scale).

Waiting time with random arrivals and **continuous** platform congestion (BPR-like):

$$
t_{e,s} = \frac{30}{f_{\text{op}}} \cdot \left(1 + \alpha\, \rho_s^{\,\beta}\right), \qquad \alpha = 0.5,\ \beta = 4
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Oferta de trenes" tag="R-5" />

with $\rho_s = L_s / (f_{\max} K)$ the station saturation. The base wait $30/f_{\text{op}} = \tfrac{1}{2 f_{\text{op}}} \cdot 60$ is the mean wait under random arrivals at frequency $f_{\text{op}}$.

## CO₂ emissions

Cars emit according to their **local circulation speed** (reconstructed by inverting the BPR per parcel):

$$
FE_{\text{auto}}(v) = 2467.4 \cdot v^{-0.699} \ \ [\text{g/km}], \qquad E_{\text{auto}} = \sum_i q_i \cdot \Delta x \cdot FE_{\text{auto}}(v_i)
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Modelo de Emisiones" tag="R-9" />

The metro emits **per train-kilometre** — the environmental cost is per service running, not per passenger:

$$
E_{\text{metro}} = \phi \cdot f_{\text{op}} \cdot L_{\text{line}} \cdot 2, \qquad \phi = 2.5\ \text{kg CO}_2/\text{train-km}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Modelo de Emisiones" tag="R-9" />

<Callout type="info">
At fixed frequency, attracting passengers to the metro does **not** raise its emissions (fixed service cost); with growing demand the frequency rises and emissions *per passenger* fall — the emissions analogue of the Mohring effect. Bike and walking are assumed emission-free.
</Callout>
