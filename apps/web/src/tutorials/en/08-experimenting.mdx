# 7. Experimenting

### B. The logit artifact
1. Under Land use, vary the high stratum's λ (0.5 → 3). Once it differs from the other two, the core switches to the heteroskedastic auction on its own.
2. Watch the allocation change — even though λ is the marginal utility of income.
3. Measured: at λ = 0.5 the high stratum sits **1.06 km** from the CBD and at λ = 3 it sits **6.59 km** away. So *raising* it pushes them out.
4. Is that an income effect? No: λ enters through α_eff = α/λ, so lowering it makes the stratum value access more. In an auction the price cancels between bidders, so λ cannot act through price sensitivity. (Hint: Land-use tutorial, correction section.)

### D. Alonso effect
1. Under Land use, set $\alpha_h$ = 12 for the high stratum and 3 for the low one (utils/min).
2. Observe the distribution. Who lives near the CBD?
3. Invert $\alpha$ values and repeat.

## Persistence

Every configuration can be exported as `.ttrq.json`:
- **Export**: share with peers or save for grading.
- **Import**: load teacher's scenario.
- **Share via link**: state is URL-compressed.
