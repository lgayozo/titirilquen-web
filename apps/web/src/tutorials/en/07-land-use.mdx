# 6. Land use

The land module (**Land use** page) decides where each household lives through an Alonso–Muth–Mills **auction**: each stratum bids for the plots and whoever values a location most wins it, pushing the rest towards the periphery. The notation is Martínez's (2018, ch. 4).

## Utility and willingness to pay

The utility of a household of stratum $h$ in plot $i$, paying rent $p_i$, is **linear in income** and has a random term:

$$
v_{hi} = \lambda_h\,(y_h - p_i) + f_h(z_i) + \varepsilon_{hi}, \qquad \varepsilon_{hi} \sim \text{Gumbel}(0, \mu)
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Función de puje" />

where $\lambda_h$ is the marginal utility of income, in utils per peso, $y_h$ the stratum's monthly income and $f_h(z_i)$ what the plot is worth by itself:

$$
f_h(z_i) = -\alpha\, T_h(i) - \rho_h \cdot \frac{S_i}{\Delta x}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Función de puje" tag="S-2" />

$T_h(i)$ is **accessibility**: the monthly cost of living in $i$ and having to travel, equal to $-44$ times the logsum of the mode-choice logit of the Demand chapter, at free flow. It is in transport utils per month and differs by stratum, since each one has its own logit. $\alpha$ equals 1 for everyone, so the bid reads that accessibility as is; $\rho_h$ penalizes **density** $S_i/\Delta x$ (households/km) and is 0 by default.

With $\lambda_h$ equal to the cost coefficient of the mode-choice logit, the same household has **a single utility function** in both modules: its value of time $\alpha/\lambda_h$ is 6,200, 3,100 and 1,600 pesos per hour for the high, middle and low strata, the same one it uses to choose a mode. That is why land use has no $\lambda$ of its own: it reads it from demand, and it moves from the **Calibration** page. Changing $b_{\text{cost}}$ moves both $\lambda_h$ and the accessibility $T_h$.

Solving for the price at the reservation utility level $u_h$ gives the **willingness to pay**, which is also random:

$$
w_{hi} = y_h + \frac{f_h(z_i) - u_h}{\lambda_h}, \qquad \theta_{hi} = w_{hi} + \frac{\varepsilon_{hi}}{\lambda_h}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Función de puje" tag="S-3" />

Dividing by $\lambda_h$ turns everything into pesos, including the noise: the bid has precision $\beta_h = \lambda_h\,\mu$. Since the $\lambda_h$ differ, **each stratum bids with different noise**: the high stratum, which values each peso less, has the widest noise in pesos.

## The auction

If all bidders for a plot had the same precision $\beta_i$, the probability that a household of stratum $h$ wins it would be a logit:

$$
Q_{h/i} = \frac{H_h\, e^{\beta_i w_{hi}}}{\sum_{g} H_g\, e^{\beta_i w_{gi}}}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Asignación probabilística" />

That only happens if the three $\lambda_h$ are equal. With the default values the precisions differ, and the simulator uses the **heteroskedastic auction** (HEV, Train §4.5): the same idea, but with an integral instead of a fraction, solved by quadrature. The factor $H_h$ (number of bidders of each type) guarantees **conservation by stratum** at equilibrium: $\sum_i S_i\, Q_{h/i} = H_h$.

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Asignación probabilística" tag="S-4" />

Equilibrium is found as a fixed point on the reservation utilities $u_h$ (each stratum places exactly its $H_h$ households). The plot's **price** is the expected maximum of the bids; with a common precision it has a closed form:

$$
p_i^{*} = \frac{1}{\beta_i} \ln\left( \sum_g H_g\, e^{\beta_i w_{gi}} \right) + \frac{\gamma}{\beta_i}
$$

<OverleafRef sec="Suelo · Interpretación del punto fijo" />

with $\gamma \approx 0.577$ Euler's constant. Prices are defined up to a constant: only the spatial **gradient** is informative.

## $\lambda$, the value of time and why the heteroskedastic auction matters

What sorts the strata in space is not $\alpha$, which is common, but **$\lambda$**: with $\alpha = 1$, the value of time $\alpha/\lambda_h$ is higher for the high stratum, which therefore bids harder for the accessibility of the center.

<Callout type="warn">
**Under the common-precision logit auction, $\lambda$ cannot be read.** The bid divides $f$ by $\lambda_h$, and $f$ is linear in $\alpha$ and $\rho$:

$$
y_h + \frac{f_h(z_i)}{\lambda_h} \;\equiv\; y_h + f\!\left(z_i;\; \frac{\alpha}{\lambda_h},\; \frac{\rho_h}{\lambda_h}\right)
$$

Moving $\lambda_h$ would be exactly rescaling that stratum's preferences. The heteroskedastic auction breaks that equivalence, because $\lambda_h$ also moves the dispersion of the bid, which rescaling preferences does not touch. That is why the simulator uses it: with it, $\lambda$ is identified. In an auction, however, $\lambda$ never acts through price sensitivity: all bidders for a plot pay the same, so the price cancels out.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
Try, on the page's default configuration:

- **Set the three $\lambda$ equal** to the middle stratum's, that is, their $b_{\text{cost}}$: in **Calibration**, set the "$s$" slider of "Value of time across strata" to 0 and rerun. The value of time becomes the same for everyone and **segregation nearly vanishes**: Theil from 0.23 to 0.02, with the high stratum 4.3 km from the CBD and the low one 3.1 km. Without differences in value of time there is no Alonso. What little remains, slightly inverted, is car ownership: the high stratum has more cars, and the car flattens its accessibility.
- **Double the high stratum's $\lambda$.** Its value of time halves and it moves away from the center: from 1.9 to 3.0 km. With $\lambda$ halved it moves closer, to 1.5 km.
- **Double the high stratum's income $y$.** It reallocates nobody: income enters as a constant and is absorbed into the reservation utility $u_h$.
</Callout>

## The precision $\mu$

$\mu$ is the module's only knob of its own: how noisy choosing a home is relative to choosing a mode. It is $1/\sqrt{44} \approx 0.15$ because accessibility is monthly, the sum of 44 trips, and if each trip carries its own shock the month's dispersion grows like $\sqrt{44}$. With $\mu = 1$ the auction is nearly deterministic and Theil segregation rises from 0.23 to 0.76.

## The shape of the city

The supply $S(i)$ is generated **deterministically** (largest-remainder discretization, $\sum_i S_i = \sum_h H_h$ exact, empty CBD) with six parameterizable shapes: normal, uniform, exponential, plateau, bimodal and valley, with dispersion $\sigma$ as a fraction of the half-city.

The **per-cell density** is a direct consequence of supply: $\text{dens}(i) = S_i/\Delta x$, so it follows the chosen shape (not an imposed gradient). The population figures and the strata heatmap share that envelope; bid-rent only reorders *who* lives where, without changing *how many* per cell.
